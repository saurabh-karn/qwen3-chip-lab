The win: keep the same computation, but make W local to the die. Then this recurring HBM weight walk disappears.

One token, four steps

Follow the only path that repeats for every generated token.

  1. 1
    Send a token ID

    The host sends a tiny token ID. The GPU turns it into activation x.

  2. 2
    Fetch the weights from HBM

    — of model weights move into the GPU for this token. This is the expensive, repeated trip.

  3. 3
    Multiply in the SMs

    — MACs combine x and W. The math is fast once the weights arrive.

  4. 4
    Repeat, then choose the next token

    Across 28 layers and the output head, the GPU keeps fetching new weights. The analytical H100 model estimates — of HBM weight-movement energy per token.

Why mask-ROM matters: the computation is unchanged, but the weights are already on the die—so step 2 no longer crosses an HBM bus.

Why the MAC waits for its weights

\( Q_j \;=\; \displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d} x_k \, W_{k,j} \qquad j = 1, \dots, d_{out} \)

This is y @ W_Q — the same reduction as an nn.Linear forward pass. Before a lane can multiply, its \(W_{k,j}\) has to arrive. In this batch 1 model, the weight matrices stream from HBM for each token. Keeping weights on the die removes that recurring transfer; compute and local-memory energy remain.

          Why MoE helps, and the invariant
          
Why MoE helps: only the fired experts are powered, so the energy bill scales with active parameters, not stored ones. The invariant: each die holds its slice of W permanently; die-to-die links carry only activations (UCIe 2.0 class, cited).           Why these levers compound on mask-ROM
          

            
Why fixed-weight ROM compounds these wins

            
Precision-aware training shapes the weights for the mask from step one: fake-quantization in the training loop, so the final checkpoint is INT4 — and the mask is that checkpoint, burnt in at fabrication. Nothing is quantized after the fact, because there is nothing to quantize: the weights are read-only silicon. The trained model and the shipped model are bit-identical. KV int8 and MLA shrink the SRAM walk on the attention die; batch amortizes the weight walk across users while KV — the GPU's growing cost — stays local.

          

          

            
What is honest about this table

            
BF16 rows are the lab path: measured cycles, analytical energy. The low-bit rows are precision-aware training — the same accuracy as BF16 by published results (Kimi K2-Thinking INT4 QAT matches BF16 on eval; DeepSeek-V3 FP8 training holds <0.25% relative error), not post-hoc approximation. There is no PTQ step to argue about: the mask is the checkpoint. MLA changes the model architecture (it is how DeepSeek-V3 ships). The ~2,000× endpoint assumes the same 40k tok/s/user and the D2D sharding from the multi-die slide. Ratios use the H100 at the same context: 26 J/token, 1.04T die transistors (HBM stacks not counted on their side).

          

        

      
          

        

      

    




    
    

      

        

          
Five ways to hold a weight, one RTL fact each

          
Every panel below shares the same ROM cell, the same BF16 MAC array, and the same bit-exact qwen-ref-v2 contract — the only variable is when and how often the weight crosses from storage into the multiplier. The fifth panel has no storage step at all.

        


        

          

            
1 · Mask-ROM, unfused

            

              
ROM2-cyc read

              
1 word, blocking

              
MAC lane1 product

            

            
One ROM request per MAC step; the lane sits idle across the full read latency before the next request fires — qwen3_forward_controller.sv, unfused schedule.

            
886.2M cyc/3-tok · 6.73 mJ

          


          

            
2 · Mask-ROM, fused/streaming

            

              
ROMstreaming

              
request every cycle

              
FIFO → MACoverlapped

            

            
Request counter decouples from the response counter, so the next word is requested before this one's response even lands — same controller, fused schedule. The 2-cycle latency is hidden, not removed.

            
171.2M cyc/3-tok · 3.41 mJ

          


          

            
3 · TPU-style, weight-stationary

            

              
ROMone tile

              
tile load, once

              
w_tile regreused rows

            

            
A resident w_tile register holds one weight row across many activation steps instead of one word per step — qwen3_tpu_engine.sv. Reuse only pays off once batch > 1; at batch=1 it's a schedule change, not a traffic cut.

            
280.3M cyc/3-tok · 3.41 mJ

          


          

            
4 · LPU-style, resident-SRAM

            

              
ROMstage once

              
whole matrix, upfront

              
w_sramzero traffic after

            

            
The entire matrix is staged into on-die SRAM before compute starts (qwen3_lpu_engine.sv, S_STAGE); once resident_q latches, ROM traffic drops to zero for the rest of the token. Staging bulk-loads are now pipelined (request/response counters decoupled, this session's fix) — still costs more upfront than streaming amortizes at batch=1.

            
402.5M cyc/3-tok · 3.41 mJ

          


          

            
5 · Fully-fused, compute-in-memory

            

              
no ROMno port, no bus

              
weight is a parameter

              
shift-add netper weight

            

            
The weight is a compile-time parameter, never a port — there is no rom_req_valid_o signal to fire at all. The multiply collapses to a shift-add network sized by that weight's own signed-digit count (qwen_maskmac_mxfp{4,8}_lane.sv, pw_bf16_core.sv): mean 0.27 adders/weight at MXFP4, 1.15 at MXFP8 — against a full multiplier for every other panel here.

            
~97× transistors/weight vs a 1-transistor ROM bit · zero weight traffic, ever

          How each row is grounded
          
measured = bit-exact RTL simulator counters (cycles, MACs, ROM reads) priced at this repo's cited N4 constants. Rows 1–4 are the identical 16-lane MAC array and ROM cell, on-die, under four request schedules — only the schedule changes. Rows 8–10 are a separate, standalone RTL spike (not wired into the main forward pass): a full-model scan over all 595,984,384 real Qwen3-0.6B weights gives the adders/weight figures per precision, and each ROM-ratio compares a 1-transistor ROM bit at that same precision (16 bits/weight BF16, ~8.25 MXFP8, ~4.25 MXFP4 including the OCP MX block scale) against that precision's measured fully-fused lane — a same-precision, apples-to-apples comparison, not all three against one BF16 baseline.
